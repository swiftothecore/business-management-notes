Week 1 · Lesson 1

Types of Businesses

Business structures available in Australia — ownership, liability, and the advantages and disadvantages of each.

Business Structure

🧑Sole Trader

  • Owned and operated by one person (may still have employees)
  • Owner makes most business decisions
  • Liability: unlimited - owner personally accountable for all debts
e.g. Owner invests $20k, business owes $50k - personal assets (home, car) can be seized to cover the debt
✔ Advantages
  • Easy and inexpensive to set up
  • Few legal/tax formalities
  • Owner has full control over assets and decisions
  • No obligation to disclose profits publicly
  • Easy to change structure or close down
✘ Disadvantages
  • Fully liable for business debts - personal assets at risk
  • Heavy reliance on owner - long work hours
  • Limited time away from the business (fewer holidays)
  • If owner is sick or injured, business may stop
Business Structure

🤝Partnership

  • Owned by 2-20 people
  • Liability: unlimited - all owners personally accountable for debts
  • Each partner liable for debts incurred by the others
✔ Advantages
  • Easy and inexpensive to set up
  • Combines resources and expertise of multiple people
  • More flexibility for holidays/sick leave than sole trader
  • No obligation to disclose profits publicly
✘ Disadvantages
  • Not a separate entity - all partners personally liable (unlimited liability)
  • Each partner liable for debts incurred by the others
  • Personal differences may interfere with business decisions
Business Structure

🏛️Private Limited Company

  • Separate legal entity - same rights as a natural person (can incur debt, sue, be sued)
  • 1-50 shareholders; shares sold privately - not listed on a stock exchange
  • Liability: limited - shareholders can only lose the value of their shares
e.g. Linfox, Wittner Shoes, Sportsgirl
✔ Advantages
  • Limited liability - shareholders can only lose share value
  • Tax advantages over sole traders and partnerships
  • Exists independently of shareholders - survives retirement/death
✘ Disadvantages
  • More complex - higher set-up and admin costs
  • More regulated than sole traders/partnerships
  • Cannot raise capital from the public (private shares only)
  • Suppliers/lenders may require personal guarantees from directors
Business Structure

📈Public Listed Company

  • Separate legal entity - same rights as a natural person (can incur debt, sue, be sued)
  • Shares sold to the public via the ASX; typically formed to raise/borrow public money
  • Shareholders: unrestricted number
  • Liability: limited - shareholders can only lose the value of their shares
e.g. Telstra, Commonwealth Bank (CBA), Wesfarmers
✔ Advantages
  • Limited liability - shareholders can only lose share value
  • Going public creates large capital influx without incurring debt
  • Can raise further funds by issuing more shares
  • Director personal guarantees not usually required for borrowing
  • ASX listing raises public awareness of the company
✘ Disadvantages
  • Complex structure - high set-up and admin costs
  • Must prioritise shareholder interests in decisions
  • Disclosure and reporting requirements apply (e.g. financial reports)
  • Share price vulnerable to market conditions regardless of performance
  • Original owners may lose control (run by board/managers)
  • Management may focus on short-term share price over long-term success
Business Type

🌿Social Enterprises

  • Exist to benefit the public/community - not shareholders or owners
  • Mission: economic, social, cultural or environmental
  • Sell goods/services and reinvest substantial profits into their mission
  • Not a legal structure - can be sole trader, partnership, etc.; structure chosen to best advance the mission
  • Liability depends on the chosen structure
  • Adv/disadv not covered in this course
e.g. The Big Issue, CERES Fair Food, Good Cycles
Business Type

🏦Government Business Enterprises (GBEs)

  • Government owned
  • Occupy strategic positions in Australia's economic and social life
  • Provide services the public market may not (communications, transport, defence)
  • Dual objective: provide a public service + make a profit
  • Profitability achieved by controlling costs and pricing goods/services accordingly
  • Not a structure entrepreneurs can choose - adv/disadv not covered in this course
e.g. Australia Post, NBN, Defence Housing Australia
Week 1 · Lesson 2

Business Objectives

Common objectives businesses pursue — from making profit and growing market share to fulfilling social needs and meeting shareholder expectations.

Business Objective

💰To Make a Profit

  • Profit = revenue minus expenses
  • Most common business objective
  • Revenue = total income from sales
  • Expenses = costs of running the business
e.g. Lemonade stall: 50 cups × $2 = $100 revenue. Expenses: $5 cups + $20 lemons/sugar + $5 stall fee = $30. Profit = $70
Business Objective

📊To Increase Market Share

  • Market share = a business's portion of an industry's total sales, expressed as a %
  • Key indicator of how well a business is performing relative to competitors
  • To increase market share = gaining sales from competitors
  • Can only be achieved by capturing share from competitors - your slice grows, theirs shrinks
Analogy: the market is a cake; every business gets a slice. Growing your market share means your slice gets bigger while someone else's gets smaller.
Business Objective

⚙️To Improve Efficiency

  • Efficiency = minimising inputs used in the production process
  • Inputs to reduce: raw materials, labour, time, electricity, waste
  • Why: creates a cost or time advantage over competitors
e.g. Supermarket: self-service checkouts → fewer staff needed
e.g. Restaurant: correct ingredient quantities → less spoilage
e.g. Sportswear manufacturer: automated production → less time per item
e.g. Aldi has built its entire business model around efficiency
Business Objective

✅To Improve Effectiveness

  • Effectiveness = how well objectives have been met
  • Achieved by implementing strategies and comparing outcomes against set objectives
Signs a business has not achieved its objectives: defective products, high customer complaints, decreased market share
e.g. Chocolate manufacturer meeting taste/quality expectations → improved sales
e.g. Restaurant with correct ingredient quantities → fewer diner complaints
Business Objective

🎯To Fulfil a Market Need

  • Market need = product or service wanted/required by a specific group of customers
  • Applies to all businesses - without a market need, there are no customers
  • Identifying and fulfilling market needs allows businesses to grow, increase profit and market share
  • Ways to fulfil a market need:
    • Satisfying existing consumer demands
    • Filling a gap in the market (new product/service in a specific area)
    • Offering a new solution to an old problem
Business Objective

🤲To Fulfil a Social Need

  • Social need = product or service wanted or required within the community
  • Focus: common good of society/local community (not just profit)
  • Range: basic services (food, shelter) through to employment/education for disadvantaged groups
  • Closely linked to social enterprises (see Lesson 1)
e.g. Backpack Bed for Homeless: sells goods to the public, uses all profits to distribute bedding to homeless people - provides a bed every 2 hours
Business Objective

📋To Meet Shareholder Expectations

  • Shareholders = persons or groups who own shares in a company (i.e. the owners)
  • Shareholders benefit in two ways:
    • Dividends: a portion of net profit paid to shareholders
    • Capital gain: selling shares at a higher price than purchase price
  • Key expectations: high dividends + rising share prices
  • Businesses implement strategies specifically to meet these expectations
e.g. Commonwealth Bank paid shareholders a dividend of $1.75 per share (March 2022)

Week 13 · Lesson 1

Strategies Related to Materials

How businesses plan, schedule, and manage the materials needed for production — from forecasting demand through to just-in-time ordering.

Materials Strategy

📈Forecasting

Predicting customer demand for upcoming periods using past data and market trends, so that production and material ordering can be planned accordingly.

  • Efficiency: Prevents both overstocking (high storage costs, wasted inputs) and understocking (production slowdowns)
  • Effectiveness: Helps the business meet demand, increasing customer satisfaction, market share, and profit
Limitations: Forecasts are not always 100% accurate. Gathering and analysing data can be time-consuming and costly.
Forecasting is often used in conjunction with a Master Production Schedule (MPS) and Materials Requirement Planning (MRP).
Materials Strategy

📅Master Production Schedule (MPS)

A detailed plan of what is to be produced and when it is to be produced.

  • Usually created using specialised software
  • Shows when the end product is needed — does not show when raw materials/components are needed (that is the role of MRP)
  • Aims to increase planning stability, provide information across the business, and reduce storage costs

👥Who uses the MPS?

  • Sales: Provides final product completion dates to promise products to customers
  • Inventory/Finance: Anticipates inventory levels of finished products
  • Operations: Drives the materials requirements planning process
Benefit: Improves effectiveness by helping the business consistently meet demand, without the chaos of responding to constantly changing orders in real time.
Materials Strategy

📋Materials Requirement Planning (MRP)

An itemised list of all the materials involved in production to meet the forecast demand.

  • Converts the MPS into a detailed purchasing list of raw materials and components
  • Usually generated using specialised software
  • Ensures the right materials are available at the right time, at the lowest possible cost

⚙️Benefits (Efficiency)

  • Prevents material shortages
  • Optimises cash flow
  • Automates the supply chain
Combined purpose of MPS and MRP: To guarantee availability of products, which satisfies customers and leads to increased profit and market share (effectiveness).
Materials Strategy

⚡Just-in-Time (JIT)

An approach to ordering materials where new materials arrive exactly when they are required in the production process — no sooner, no later.

✔ Advantages
  • Minimises storage costs (efficiency)
  • Less capital tied up in stock — freed up for other uses (efficiency)
  • Reduces the likelihood of materials being wasted due to damage or spoilage in storage (efficiency)
  • Lowers costs and increases profits (effectiveness)
✘ Disadvantages
  • Business becomes heavily dependent on suppliers — supply chain disruptions can cause production slowdowns or stops
  • Difficult to respond to sudden, unexpected increases in demand (insufficient materials on hand)
  • Little tolerance for mistakes, as minimal stock is kept for re-producing faulty products
Week 13 · Lesson 2

Strategies Related to Quality

Three approaches businesses use to manage and improve the quality of their goods or services — from basic inspection through to a whole-business commitment to excellence.

Quality Strategy

🔍Quality Control

Involves the use of inspections at various points in the production process to check for problems and defects. If defects are found, corrective action is taken so that customers receive goods or services of high quality.

  • Establish quality standards
  • Carry out inspections at different stages of production
  • Compare results with set standards
  • Take corrective action if necessary
✔ Advantages
  • Low quality products are likely to be detected before reaching the customer
  • Simple to establish and inexpensive
  • Standards can be adjusted over time to meet changing conditions (e.g. rising customer expectations)
✘ Disadvantages
  • Reactive — by the time a fault is discovered, the low quality product has already been made
  • Takes time to conduct regular checks
Quality Strategy

🏅Quality Assurance

Involves an independent external business that audits against national or international standards. If standards are met, the business gains the right to display the external provider's logo.

e.g. International Organisation for Standardisation (ISO)
✔ Advantages
  • External standards prevent internal manipulation of results
  • Proactive — the goal is to avoid errors before they occur
  • Regular audits ensure continuous improvement
✘ Disadvantages
  • Can be time-consuming
  • Costs money for certification, implementation, and audits
  • Costs money to train staff
Quality Strategy

⭐Total Quality Management (TQM)

An ongoing, business-wide commitment to excellence shared by all employees. Aims to create a defect-free production process with a focus on the customer.

💪Employee Empowerment

Quality is the responsibility of every employee. Employee participation in decision-making encourages everyone to take ownership of quality, with each employee aiming to eliminate errors in their part of the process.

🔄Continuous Improvement

An ongoing commitment to becoming better in all aspects of the business, through constantly raising standards in pursuit of perfection.

🎯Customer Focus

Quality is defined by customer expectations. All employees must understand their target customers and consistently aim to satisfy their needs and wants.

✔ Advantages
  • All staff and management share ownership and responsibility for quality
  • Every aspect of the business is continually assessed
✘ Disadvantages
  • Can be difficult to allocate responsibility for errors
  • Relies on all staff being motivated and highly skilled
  • Requires strong communication skills to be effective

Lean Management

A production philosophy focused on eliminating waste by aligning output precisely to customer demand. Core principles: Pull, One-Piece Flow, Takt, and Zero Defects.

Lean Principle

←Pull

  • Production driven by actual customer demand - product is pulled backwards through manufacturing from the customer's order
  • Contrasts with push: push produces to forecasted demand, leaving goods in inventory until sold
  • Avoids overproduction and stockpiling, both of which create waste
  • Components replaced only once used - all resources go to goods that will actually sell
✔ Advantages
  • Low inventories - reduced storage costs and waste
  • Sale of produced units guaranteed (based on actual demand)
  • Suitable for unpredictable sales volumes
  • Increases flexibility - fast response to changes in demand
✘ Disadvantages
  • Heavily dependent on suppliers - supply chain disruptions can halt production entirely
Lean Principle

→One-Piece Flow

  • Only one item is at any given stage of production at one time
  • Contrasts with mass/batch production, where groups of items move together - inefficient due to task-switching and unnecessary movement
  • Multiple items can exist in the overall process simultaneously, but only one at each workstation
✔ Advantages
  • Errors detected earlier - focus on one item improves quality
  • Increased speed - eliminates waiting time and unnecessary movement
  • Reduces inventory costs
✘ Disadvantages
  • Expensive to reconfigure the production line
  • Employee motivation may decrease due to monotonous, repetitive tasks
  • Not suitable for high-variety products
  • Not suitable for products requiring batch processing
Lean Principle

⏱Takt

  • Takt time = the rate at which a business must complete a product to meet customer demand
  • Applies to both manufacturing and service businesses
Formula: Takt time = Available production time ÷ Units required
⚠ Slower than takt time
  • Backlog occurs - customer demand cannot be met
⚠ Faster than takt time
  • Staff and machinery underutilised
  • Producing more than needed causes overproduction
Lean Principle

✦Zero Defects

  • "Do it right the first time" - avoid and reduce errors to prevent costly corrections later
  • Defects found at one stage must be detected before passing to the next
✔ Advantages
  • Reduces cost and waste
  • Increases customer satisfaction - potentially improves sales and profit
  • Creates a culture of continuous improvement
✘ Disadvantages
  • Unrealistic to expect zero defects entirely
  • Dedicating excessive resources to it can be a poor use of those resources
  • Increased pressure on staff can negatively impact morale
Week 14 · Lesson 2

Global Considerations

Strategies businesses use to operate beyond their home country — sourcing inputs globally, manufacturing overseas, and outsourcing functions to foreign businesses.

Global Strategy

🌐Global Sourcing of Inputs

  • Obtaining inputs (raw materials, parts) from suppliers outside the home country
✔ Advantages
  • Lower costs: overseas manufacturing costs often cheaper → reduces prices, increases competitiveness and profit (efficiency + effectiveness)
  • Access to inputs not available locally: parts, machinery, equipment, world-class technology, specialised knowledge
  • Overseas inputs may be of higher quality than local alternatives
✘ Disadvantages
  • Supply chain interruptions: inputs may not arrive as planned → slows or stops production; backup plans required
  • Quality harder to monitor: if inputs don't meet standard, replacement takes longer due to shipping times
  • Must ensure suppliers meet CSR and OH&S expectations — fair wages, safe workplaces, environmental protection
Global Strategy

🏭Overseas Manufacture

  • Production of a good located in a different country to the business's headquarters
✔ Advantages
  • Lower costs: labour and production costs cheaper overseas, government regulations often less strict (= efficiency) → lower prices, greater competitiveness and profit (= effectiveness)
  • If products are sold overseas, manufacturing locally to that market can speed up delivery
✘ Disadvantages
  • Jobs lost domestically: impacts employees, families and community; damages business reputation; customers may prefer locally made products
  • If outputs are sold back home: shipping costs, timing and interruptions must be managed; shipping also has an environmental impact
  • Significant initial set-up costs; obtaining permits from foreign governments is time intensive and expensive
Global Strategy

🔗Global Outsourcing

  • Transferring some business functions to external, overseas businesses
  • Business runs a tender and selects another business to complete a specific task
  • Typically done to reduce costs, improve quality, or improve productivity
e.g. Website development outsourced to Asia (lower labour costs); call centres for telecoms/finance outsourced to India or the Philippines
✔ Advantages
  • Tender process allows selection of the best available business at the best price
  • Outsourcing to specialists frees the business to focus on core activities
  • If the relationship fails, ending the contract is simpler than closing overseas manufacturing (no own staff hired, no HR process required)
✘ Disadvantages
  • Loss of control over the production process; sensitive business information must be shared with third parties
  • Language and cultural barriers can cause issues
  • Time intensive to find the right partner business

Waste Management: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

Strategies that improve efficiency and effectiveness of operations through waste minimisation in the production process, going above what is legally required.

Waste Management

♻️Reduce, Reuse, Recycle — Overview

  • 'Reduce, reuse, recycle' are strategies used to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of operations through waste minimisation in the production process
  • These strategies go above and beyond what is legally required — an example of acting in a socially responsible manner
  • Reduce — lower the amount of waste generated
  • Reuse — find ways to use items again rather than discarding them
  • Recycle — convert waste material into something new and useful instead of sending it to landfill
Waste Management

📉Reduce

  • Definition: lowering the amount of waste generated during operations
  • Improves efficiency by cutting unnecessary resource use, reducing costs
  • Improves effectiveness by demonstrating CSR, which can attract customers and improve reputation
e.g. A café uses washable cups, plates and cutlery instead of disposable items. Offices go paperless and print double-sided. Customers encouraged to bring their own shopping bags. Lights and electrical equipment switched off when not in use.
Waste Management

🔄Reuse

  • Definition: repeated use of items and finding ways to use things that would otherwise be thrown out
  • Improves efficiency by reducing spending on new materials, lowering input costs
  • Improves effectiveness by minimising waste in the production process, supporting CSR goals
e.g. Packaging materials (boxes, bubble wrap) that arrive with supplier inputs are reused when selling products to consumers, rather than purchasing new packaging.
Waste Management

🗑️Recycle

  • Definition: turning waste material into something new and useful instead of sending it to landfill
  • Improves efficiency by diverting waste that would otherwise incur disposal costs
  • Improves effectiveness by reducing environmental impact and supporting CSR, which can increase market share
e.g. Soft plastic packaging turned into picnic benches, playground equipment and recycling bins. Textiles (clothing, bedding) in poor condition converted into cleaning rags or insulation rather than sent to landfill.
Week 12 · Lesson 2

Strategies Related to Technological Developments

Six technology-based strategies businesses use to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of their operations. Each can be evaluated by its advantages, disadvantages, and overall fit for a given scenario.

Technology Strategy

🏭Automated Production Lines

  • Definition: multiple workstations linked by a transfer system and electrical control system; each station performs a specific operation, with timing and sequencing controlled by computers
  • Best suited to high-volume (mass) production of goods with stable product designs — cannot accommodate frequent design changes
  • Employee input is reduced to designing systems, making adjustments, and supervising — not performing production tasks directly
  • Improves efficiency by speeding up production through repetitive sequencing and technology rather than human labour; economies of scale lower unit costs
  • Improves effectiveness by producing goods of consistent quality, satisfying customers and helping the business to make a profit
✔ Advantages
  • High production rate, reduced production times
  • Lower labour costs — less direct labour required
  • Lower unit costs — economies of scale from buying raw materials in bulk
  • Consistent quality; greater precision and accuracy
  • Occupational health and safety (OH&S) benefits — dangerous tasks performed by machines
  • Replaces repetitive and tedious tasks
✘ Disadvantages
  • High initial set-up costs; ongoing maintenance costs
  • Layout is relatively hard to change once built
  • CSR consideration: removal of human employment opportunity
Technology Strategy

🤖Robotics

  • Definition: the design, construction, operation and application of machines (robots) to perform tasks traditionally done by people
  • Used in manufacturing for repetitive, dangerous, or intricate tasks; often used within an automated production line
  • Also increasingly used to provide services (e.g. in hotels, hospitality)
  • Improves efficiency by speeding up the manufacturing process, reducing unit costs, and increasing accuracy — eliminating possible waste
  • Improves effectiveness by producing products of consistent quality, satisfying customers and helping the business make a profit and grow market share
✔ Advantages
  • Speeds up the manufacturing process
  • Increases accuracy, reduces errors
  • Can perform tasks that are dangerous for humans
  • Can work without needing breaks
✘ Disadvantages
  • Costly to set up (hardware, software, training)
  • Requires specialised knowledge and skills
  • Attracts ongoing maintenance costs
Technology Strategy

📐Computer-Aided Design (CAD)

  • Definition: computer software that enables engineers and architects to design and modify products, create precision drawings or technical illustrations, and document the design process
  • A standard tool for manufacturing and design businesses; used for new products, packaging, buildings, and product components
  • Improves efficiency by speeding up the product design process; allows the product to be tested virtually before production begins, reducing errors and eliminating possible waste
  • Improves effectiveness by calculating manufacturing costs during the design phase so changes can be made to maximise profit; 3D modelling lets clients request changes, increasing customer satisfaction and market share
✔ Advantages
  • Speeds up product design — faster to draw and modify than manual methods
  • Increases accuracy, reduces errors
  • Can test the product before starting production
  • Businesses and clients can collaborate on the design
  • 3D modelling provides clients with a realistic preview
✘ Disadvantages
  • Costly to set up (hardware, software, training)
  • Computers are prone to breakdowns, viruses and security breaches
  • Need to keep up with ever-changing and improving technology
Technology Strategy

⚙️Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM)

  • Definition: the use of software to control the manufacturing process — machinery, tools and equipment are operated via a computer
  • Often used after CAD: CAD drawings are linked to CAM machines to produce objects directly from designs
  • Widely used when a highly complex component or a large number of identical products are needed (e.g. automotive, electronics)
  • The process can be repeated to exact specifications — a level of precision impossible with hand-controlled tools
  • Improves efficiency by speeding up manufacturing and reducing unit costs; high accuracy eliminates wastage
  • Improves effectiveness by producing consistent quality products that satisfy customers, helping the business make a profit and grow market share
✔ Advantages
  • Speeds up manufacturing, reducing unit costs
  • High accuracy — less wastage, all products identical
  • Can manufacture complex parts that would be very difficult or impossible via manual machine control
✘ Disadvantages
  • High initial set-up costs, including employee training
  • Ongoing cost of staying up to date with new hardware and software
  • Time intensive to write the program to exact business and production requirements
Technology Strategy

🧠Artificial Intelligence (AI)

  • Definition: a range of smart technologies able to perform tasks that usually require human intelligence and that have the ability to learn and improve on their own
  • Can analyse data, detect patterns, and automate boring, repetitive, and time-consuming tasks
  • Improves efficiency by speeding up processes through technology rather than human labour, and automating operations to reduce costs
  • Improves effectiveness by producing goods and services of consistent quality, satisfying customers and helping the business make a profit
Manufacturing: increases accuracy and product quality, controls production rates, automates inventory forecasting and ordering.
Banking: detects fraud, processes simple loan applications, matches investment products to customers.
Chatbots: answer routine customer questions online, freeing staff for more complex tasks — reducing labour costs.
✔ Advantages
  • Drives innovation — uncovers opportunities for new products, services and business growth
  • Boosts efficiency through process automation
  • Increased precision and accuracy improves effectiveness
  • More consistent and higher quality outputs
  • Can reduce labour costs as staff are redeployed or replaced
  • Creates demand for highly skilled roles to maintain AI systems
✘ Disadvantages
  • Data trustworthiness concerns — individual rights at risk if data is lost, stolen or misused
  • Replaces human workers, removing jobs from employees who may lack the skills to keep pace
  • High cost of training existing workforce to operate AI systems
  • Every stage of AI implementation is costly: development, set-up, running, and ongoing maintenance all require significant investment and computing power
Technology Strategy

🌐Online Services

  • Definition: activities and processes that a business may offer over the internet — focus is on improving the operations system, not marketing or sales
  • Improves efficiency by enabling staff to be allocated where needed as demand arises; online bookings require fewer operators; reduces costs of running a physical shopfront
  • Improves effectiveness by providing greater business opportunities and competitiveness, increasing market share; convenience of online services can increase sales and profit
Study tip: Focus on how online services improve the operations system, not marketing and sales. Answers that only discuss marketing are not acceptable.
Examples

💡Examples of Online Services

  • Websites and mobile applications — customers order online, initiating the operations system; 'click and collect' allows optimal use of limited resources; production based on actual orders reduces overproduction and wastage
  • Booking systems — used in restaurants, hotels, hairdressers, medical appointments; allows businesses to allocate staff and resources accordingly
  • Customer feedback — via website or QR code; leads to changes in operations and improvements in product and service quality
  • Online training — self-paced via intranet or websites; increases staff knowledge and skills, leading to operations improvements
  • Cloud computing — remote servers store and process data; accessible anywhere, anytime; enables collaboration and speeds up processes
  • Website for suppliers — improves management of supplier relationships and communication, reducing delays in supply
✔ Advantages
  • Increased speed of delivery
  • Greater convenience to customers — services and bookings available online
  • Cheaper — reduced need for physical stores; reduced staffing
  • Quick and easy for customers to provide quality feedback
✘ Disadvantages
  • Initial cost of establishing a website can be expensive
  • Ongoing maintenance costs — particularly to ensure adequate security of customer data
  • Websites can crash, causing disruptions to the operations system
  • Delivery of physical goods purchased online adds costs and environmental impact from transportation
