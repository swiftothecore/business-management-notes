Week 1

Types of Businesses

An overview of the main business structures available in Australia, including their ownership, liability, and the advantages and disadvantages of each.

Business Structure

🧑Sole Trader

A sole trader is a business that is owned and operated by one person. The owner generally makes most of the decisions about running the business, but may have employees.

The liability is unlimited. This means that the owner can be held personally accountable for a business's debt.

Example: Susan is a sole trader and has invested $20,000 in her café. The business accrues debts of $50,000. If the business is unable to repay the debt, Susan's personal assets (like her car or her house) can be seized.
✔ Advantages
  • Easy and inexpensive to set up.
  • Few legal and tax formalities.
  • The owner has full control over the assets and the business decisions.
  • It is not necessary to disclose the profits to the public.
  • It is relatively easy to change the structure when the business grows, and it is also easy to close the business.
✘ Disadvantages
  • The sole trader is fully liable for the business debts and risks losing personal assets (home, vehicles, etc.), if the business cannot repay the debts.
  • Heavy reliance on the owner, resulting in long work hours.
  • It is usually limited as to how long the owner can stay away from the business (i.e. fewer holidays).
  • If the sole trader becomes sick or has an accident, the business may stop operating.
Business Structure

🤝Partnership

Partnerships are businesses that are owned by between 2 and 20 people.

The liability is unlimited. This means that the owners can be held personally accountable for a business's debt.

✔ Advantages
  • A partnership is relatively easy and inexpensive to set up.
  • It combines the resources and expertise of a number of people.
  • It allows for greater flexibility in holidays and sick leave (compared to a sole trader structure).
  • Like a sole trader, it is not necessary to disclose profits to the public.
✘ Disadvantages
  • The business is not a separate entity, and all partners are personally responsible for the business debts (unlimited liability). Each partner is individually liable for debts incurred by the other partners.
  • Personal differences between the owners may interfere with business decisions.
Business Structure

🏛️Private Limited Company

A company is a separate legal entity. This means that the company has the same rights as a natural person and can incur debt, sue and be sued.

A private limited company is a company with 1 to 50 shareholders, with shares being sold privately — i.e. it is not listed on a stock exchange.

The liability is limited: shareholders can only lose the value of their shares and are not liable for the company's debts.
Examples: Linfox, Wittner Shoes and Sportsgirl.
✔ Advantages
  • The limited liability is an advantage as the shareholders can only lose the value of their shares.
  • This structure has tax advantages compared to sole traders and partnerships.
  • The business is a separate legal entity, which means that it can exist beyond the life of its shareholders (e.g. retirement or death).
✘ Disadvantages
  • It is a more complex structure with higher set-up and administrative costs.
  • Companies are more regulated than sole traders or partnerships.
  • A private limited company cannot raise money from the general public through share issues, as the shares are only being sold privately.
  • Suppliers and lenders may be reluctant to enter into contracts or lend money unless directors or shareholders provide personal guarantees.
Business Structure

📈Public Listed Company

A company is a separate legal entity. This means that the company has the same rights as a natural person and can incur debt, sue and be sued.

A public listed company offers shares for sale to the public via the Australian Stock Exchange (ASX). A public listed company is usually formed to raise or borrow public money.

The number of shareholders is not restricted. The liability is limited: shareholders can only lose the value of their shares and are not liable for the company's debts.
Examples: Telstra, Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA) and Wesfarmers.
✔ Advantages
  • The limited liability is an advantage as the shareholders can only lose the value of their shares.
  • When businesses go public they typically sell large blocks of new shares, creating a large influx of capital at one time without incurring debt.
  • It is possible to raise additional funds by selling more shares.
  • Personal guarantees of the directors are not usually required for borrowings.
  • The market develops greater awareness of the company and its products and services through the listing on the ASX.
✘ Disadvantages
  • It is a complex structure with high set-up and administrative costs.
  • It is necessary to consider shareholder interests when running the company.
  • Disclosure and reporting requirements apply, e.g. financial reports.
  • The price of the shares may be affected by market conditions no matter how well the company is run.
  • The original owners potentially lose control as the public listed company gets run by the board of directors and managers, meaning disagreements are possible.
  • Managers might focus on short-term share prices instead of long-term success of the business.
Business Type

🌿Social Enterprises

Social enterprises are businesses that exist primarily to benefit the public and the community, rather than their shareholders and owners. They are led by an economic, social, cultural or environmental mission consistent with a public or community benefit.

Social enterprises sell goods and services to reinvest a substantial part of their profit to fulfil their mission.

Social enterprise is not a business structure like sole trader — the business structure depends on what will best advance the social mission. Sole trader, partnership and other options are all possible. The liability will depend on the chosen business structure.

As this is not a legal business structure, advantages and disadvantages will not be considered in this course.

Examples: The Big Issue, CERES Fair Food and Good Cycles.
Business Type

🏦Government Business Enterprises (GBEs)

A government business enterprise (GBE) is a business that is government owned. GBEs occupy strategic positions in the economic and social life of Australia: they provide a range of services, including communications, transport and defence (which might not be provided via the public market).

Their objectives are to provide a service or product to the public and to make a profit. GBEs seek to run profitably by controlling costs and selling their goods and services at a price to cover costs.

As this is not a business structure that an entrepreneur can choose, advantages and disadvantages will not be considered in this course.

Examples: Australia Post, National Broadband Network (NBN) and Defence Housing Australia.
